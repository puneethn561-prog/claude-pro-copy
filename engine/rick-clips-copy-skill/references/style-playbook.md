Source: the 7 user-supplied reference Shorts in `style-refs/` (analysed 2026-11-23: faster-whisper small.en word
timestamps, frame-difference cut detection, 1 fps contact sheets). These are **techniques and templates only**.
Never copy a reference's script, sentences, jokes, characters or footage (a 2-3 word fragment below is only there to
illustrate a pattern). The Anti-Whiteout rules in SKILL.md still win: the refs use white round heads and white
props, we do NOT - real skin tones, no #FFFFFF anywhere, captions with a thick dark stroke.

shot length (1.4-2.2 s), and cut the number of escalation beats, not the speed.

## 1. Script rules

### 1.1 Hook formulas (line 1, spoken from 0.0 s, 2-4 s long, 8-17 words)
Every ref starts on narration at 0.0 s - no intro, no title, no silence. Line 1 is a **past-tense strange action by
an unnamed "He"**, stated as fact, which is secretly the story's climax/solution told out of context. The viewer
can't see why anyone would do it -> curiosity gap.

Templates (fill with our own story):
- **Odd act + specific number:** "He [did odd physical thing] [exact number] [unit]." (e.g. "...82 holes...", "...every 39 feet")
- **Odd act on the obvious wrong choice:** "He [bet / bet everything / chose] the [worst-looking option]."
- **They did something absurd to a serious thing:** "They [made the serious thing silly/strange], and not just any [x]."
- **Odd act + instant consequence in the same breath:** "He [did reckless thing], and [bad result happens]."
- **Odd act + a threat breathing down his neck:** "He [calm odd act] with [danger] so close [sensory detail]."

Rules:
- Start with "He/They" + a concrete, filmable verb (bent, poured, painted, sawed, drilled, bet, slammed).
- Include one concrete detail that sounds wrong (a number, a colour, "the slowest", "cooking oil").
- No question, no "Did you know", no topic name up front. The invention/answer is never named in line 1.
- Line 2 starts with a hinge ("You see," / "because" / "The whole room is laughing...") and gives the **stakes or the
  rival** within ~5-8 s. The premise is fully clear by ~8-12 s (12-18% of runtime).

### 1.2 Story beat template (percent of runtime; seconds for a 35 s reel)
| # | beat | % of runtime | 35 s reel | what happens |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cold-open hook | 0-5% | 0-2 s | the strange act (climax out of context) |
| 2 | Stakes / setup | 5-18% | 2-6 s | why it matters: race, rival, army, bet, money; who laughs at him |
| 3 | Doubt / mockery | 18-30% | 6-10 s | boss/crowd/rival mocks him in a 3-7 word quoted line; he doesn't explain |
| 4 | Escalation 1-2 | 30-55% | 10-19 s | the world seems to prove him wrong (rival wins, everyone switches, it rains, more crashes) |
| 5 | Turn | 55-70% | 19-25 s | "Then..." / "Until one..." / "On the hottest day..." - the condition arrives |
| 6 | Reveal / mechanism | 65-85% | 23-30 s | "You see," + one everyday analogy (car on a hairpin, blowing on soup) told in 2-3 sentences |
| 7 | Payoff + loop | 85-100% | 30-35 s | rival/villain gets the consequence, hero is fine; last words flow into line 1 |

Variants seen: the reveal can come BEFORE the payoff (mechanism, then "So when..." the disaster lands), or the reel can
skip the explanation and end on the disaster cliffhanger (railroad ref), or the last line names the invention
("And that's how the [X] was invented.").

### 1.3 Cause -> effect chain
- One protagonist from line 1 to the end, almost never named ("He", "the owner", "the youngest engineer").
- One antagonist/foil: a rival, a boss, a crowd, the insurance company, a pirate ship.
- Every sentence starts with a chain word. Frequency in the refs: **But** (most), **So**, **Then**, **And**,
  **You see,**, **Until**, **Meanwhile**, **By [time]**, **Eventually**, **That's when**.
- Time progression is explicit and stamped: "the next week", "by the fifth race", "In May... By July...",
  "within a year", "one rainy morning", "At 15 minutes... At 20...". Use a 3-step ladder for slow builds.
- One "Wait, what?" style 2-4 word beat right after the most surprising event (~50-55%), then "Well, ..." answer.
- 1-3 short quoted dialogue lines from side characters (4-9 words, sarcastic). The hero almost never explains himself.

### 1.4 Line-length and pace rules
- Speech rate 3.5-3.8 words/s, gaps between sentences 0.2-0.4 s, no long pauses.
- Sentence length: mean 11-15 words, range 1-25. Mix: one long setup sentence (18-25 words), then a punch (2-7 words:
  "Not really.", "But it's too late.", "Rail touching rail."). Never two long sentences without a short one after.
- Two refs are written as one breathless run-on chain - fine for action stories, still with chain words.
- The narration runs to the very last frame (tail after last word 0.1-0.4 s). No outro, no "subscribe".

### 1.5 Ending / loop rules
- The final sentence is a payoff that (a) completes the image line 1 started, and (b) ends on a word that sets up
  line 1 grammatically or visually, so on replay "...[last word]. He [hook act]..." reads as cause and effect.
- Ending shapes: villain gets his own trap; crowd stops laughing; "the exact opposite" twist; "that's how X was
  invented"; cliffhanger mid-disaster.
- Last image = hero in foreground, calm/smug, consequence (explosion, money, crash) behind him.

## 2. Camera / shot rules

### 2.1 Cut rhythm
- Average shot 1.4-2.2 s; action climaxes go to 0.4-0.8 s cuts (rocket launch, wave, crash), mockery/dialogue holds
  2.5-3.5 s. Longest single shot ~4-5 s (a slow scenic crane during setup).
- **Cut on the word**: cuts land within ~0.1 s of a word onset, on (a) the noun that names what the new shot shows
  ("loop", "holes", "army", "mirror"), or (b) the sentence's chain word ("But", "So", "Then", "Meanwhile").
- Transitions are 2-4 frame motion-blurred whips/smears or very short cross-blurs - never a slow dissolve, never a hard
  black. One hard cut to an iris/spyglass POV is fine.
- The camera NEVER stops: every shot has a move plus a slow drift; even dialogue shots push in slowly.

### 2.2 Shot grammar table (beat -> camera move -> duration)
| beat | camera move | duration | framing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook (0 s) | fast pull-back/crane-up from the hero mid-action to reveal the odd object | 1-1.5 s | MCU -> MS, hero + prop |
| Hook word 2 | whip to the object itself, low angle, slight orbit | 1-1.5 s | prop fills 60-80% |
| Stakes | big establishing crane / drone fly-over / tilt-down into the drop (canyon, sea, valley) | 2-4 s | wide, hero tiny or absent |
| Rival / crowd | tracking dolly along the line of people, or push-in on the rival | 1.5-2.5 s | MS, 2-4 people |
| Mockery line | slow push-in on the speaker, 2-shot over the hero's shoulder | 2-3.5 s | MCU, head 30-40% of width |
| Process / prep | macro insert top-down or low (paint pour, drilling, wiring, holes), slow orbit | 1-2 s | ECU on hands/prop |
| Time passing | calendar/sign/counter insert, or same angle with lighting change (day->dusk->night->rain) | 1-1.5 s | insert |
| Escalation | tracking shot alongside vehicles/runners, POV from vehicle, Dutch tilt | 1-2 s | MS-wide |
| "Wait, what?" | quick punch-in / crash zoom on a reacting face | 0.5-1 s | CU |
| Reveal analogy | cut to a NEW simple set (kitchen, road, test bench); top-down on the analogy object; diagram-like | 1.5-3 s | insert/medium |
| Payoff | POV ride (first-person on the cart/train), then a wide of the disaster, then low hero shot | 0.5-1.5 s each | POV -> wide -> MS |
| Last frame | low-angle slow push on smug hero, consequence behind | 1-2 s | MS, hero centred |

Also used: whip pans between sets, orbit around the key prop, dolly-zoom-like push while background slides, POV through
a spyglass (circular iris), screen-within-screen (CCTV monitor, dashcam HUD with REC/date/MPH as diegetic overlay),
tilt reveal from object up to face, top-down on a crowd/army, map-like aerial over a country for "everywhere" beats.

### 2.3 Framing rules
- Mix per reel (approx): 45-55% character medium/close, 20-25% wide/establishing/environment, 20-30% inserts/macro/POV.
- Medium shots: character full head + torso, head ~25-35% of frame width, eyes around 30-40% from top. Close-ups:
  head 40-60% of width, still never cropped at the top (our rule: hat included).
- Wides: hero small (5-15% of height) inside a huge, detailed set; strong depth (rails, roads, walls leading to horizon).
- Keep faces and the key prop ABOVE the caption band (caption sits ~68-72% down the frame).
- Each act changes set or lighting: e.g. day yard -> night storeroom -> rainy road -> office -> sunset hill.
- Faces always act: smug half-lidded eyes for the hero, angry (even colour-shifted face, steam puffs) for the boss,
  sweat drops, laughing crowds. Side characters fill the background of most mediums.

### 2.4 On-screen stamps / labels
- No floating graphic stamps in the refs; all labels are **diegetic**: signs, banners, newspaper headlines, odds
  boards, calendars, day counters, cans with labels, paint-swatch posters, HUD of a camera.
- Each label is 1-4 words, big, readable whole, and appears on the exact word that names it.
- (Our skill may still use stamps on reveal words, but prefer an in-world sign/headline/counter when the set allows.)

### 2.5 Caption rules (as seen)
- 1 word per caption most of the time, 2 words when a short function word leads ("the walls", "a coin", "his buddy").
- Changes on every word, exactly in sync with the voice.
- Position: horizontally centred, ~68-72% down the frame (lower-middle), same spot every shot.
- Size: modest - cap height ~3-3.5% of frame height (~60-70 px on 1920). Bold rounded sans, white with dark
  stroke + soft shadow. (Our Anti-Whiteout rule: stroke >= 6 px at 1080x1920.) No colour-highlighting, no emoji.
- Numbers shown as digits in captions.

### 2.6 Sound rules
- Narration starts at 0.0 s and ends at the last frame; one deep, calm, slightly amused storyteller voice.
- A continuous music bed under everything (non-speech level only ~5-8 dB below the voice) - it never drops out.
- SFX on cuts and actions: whooshes on whips, impacts on crashes/slams, crowd laughter, rain, clicking wire, explosions;
  louder spikes reserved for the climax.
- Quoted lines are read by the same narrator with a slight character voice.

## 4. Planning checklist (use for every new script + shot list)
1. Line 1 = "He/They + odd filmable act + one wrong-sounding detail", 0.0 s, <= 17 words, no question.
2. Premise + stakes clear by ~15% of runtime; a mocking foil by ~25%.
3. Every sentence opens with a chain word; a time ladder stamps progress.
4. One 2-4 word "Wait, what?" beat near the middle.
5. Reveal = "You see," + one everyday analogy, 2-3 sentences, at 65-85%.
6. Payoff: foil gets the consequence; last words loop into line 1; narration to the last frame.
7. Shots 1.4-2.2 s avg, cut on the naming noun or chain word, 2-4 frame whip blur, camera always moving.
8. ~50% character MS/CU, ~25% wides, ~25% inserts/macro/POV; one POV ride or screen-within-screen per reel.
9. Labels are in-world signs/counters/headlines on the word that names them.
10. Captions 1-2 words, centred ~70% down, modest size, white + thick dark stroke; nothing important under them.
